Prism

Rainbow

Thousands of rays, one rule

Each light fires hundreds of rays, and every ray carries a single colour. When a ray meets glass it bends by Snell's law:

n1 · sin θ1 = n2 · sin θ2

Glass slows violet light more than red, so each colour bends by a slightly different amount. That is why white light entering a prism leaves as a rainbow, and why a simple lens can't focus every colour at the same point.

A little light also reflects off every glass surface, and past a critical angle all of it does: total internal reflection, the trick behind fibre optics. The dispersion here is exaggerated so it's easy to see.
