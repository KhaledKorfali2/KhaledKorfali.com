Ink

A real fluid, solved on your graphics card

The screen is a grid of cells, and every cell stores how fast the fluid there is moving. Many times a second the grid is updated by the equations that describe real liquids and gases:

∂u/∂t = −(u·∇)u − ∇p + forces,   ∇·u = 0

In words: the fluid carries itself along, and it can't be squashed. That second rule is what makes it curl. Push fluid forward and the fluid beside it has to move out of the way and round behind, which is where the swirls come from.

Your finger adds a push and a drop of dye. The dye is just carried by the flow, so what you are seeing is the motion itself.

The Swirl slider feeds energy back into small whirlpools that the grid would otherwise smooth away.

Drag through it. Tap for a burst.
This one needs a graphics feature (float textures in WebGL) that this browser or device doesn't offer.
