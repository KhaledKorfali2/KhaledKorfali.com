# Who did what

A sentence that starts with a verb is called a **verbal sentence**. Its usual order is verb, then the one who did it, then what it was done to. This is where the case endings from lesson 8 do their work.

## Doer and object

- The **doer** is in the u-case.
- The **object** is in the a-case.

> خَلَقَ اللَّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ
> Allah created the heavens and the earth. (29:44)

اللَّهُ ends in u: the doer. الْأَرْضَ ends in a: an object. السَّمَاوَاتِ is an object too, but plurals ending in ـَات show the a-case with an i, so it ends in i.

> وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا
> And He taught Adam the names, all of them. (2:31)

Here the doer isn't written as a separate word. It is inside the verb: عَلَّمَ already means "he taught".

## The endings matter more than the order

Because the endings mark the roles, the words can be rearranged for emphasis. This verse is the classic example:

> إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى اللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ الْعُلَمَاءُ
> Only those with knowledge among His servants truly fear Allah. (35:28)

The name of Allah comes straight after the verb, but it ends in **a**, so it is the object. الْعُلَمَاءُ, "the knowledgeable", comes last but ends in **u**, so it is the doer. Reading by word order alone would turn the meaning upside down.

## The verb stays singular

When the doer is written after the verb, the verb stays singular even if the doer is plural.

> قَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا
> Those who disbelieved said ...

قَالَ is singular, although "those who disbelieved" are many.

## Cards

Q: What are the cases of the doer and the object of a verb?
A: The doer (فَاعِلٌ) is in the u-case. The object (مَفْعُولٌ بِهِ) is in the a-case.

Q: In إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى اللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ الْعُلَمَاءُ (35:28), who fears whom, and how do you know?
A: The knowledgeable fear Allah. الْعُلَمَاءُ ends in u, so it is the doer. اللَّهَ ends in a, so it is the object.
