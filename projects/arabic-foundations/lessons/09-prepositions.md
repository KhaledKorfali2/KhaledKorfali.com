# Prepositions

Prepositions are particles such as "in", "from" and "on". A small set of them accounts for a large share of all the words in the Qur'an, so they repay learning early.

## The rule

**The noun after a preposition is always in the i-case.**

> فِي الْأَرْضِ
> in the earth

> مِنْ رَبِّهِمْ
> from their Lord

The preposition itself never changes.

## Three that are joined on

بِـ, لِـ and كَـ are a single letter each, so they are written attached to the next word.

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ
> In the name of Allah. بِـ joined to اسْم, "name". (1:1)

> لِلَّهِ
> for Allah, belonging to Allah

When لِـ meets الـ, the alif is dropped in writing, which is why لِلَّهِ has no alif.

## Meanings shift with context

Each preposition has a main meaning and several others. بِـ is most often "with" or "by", but can also be "in". عَلَى is "on", but with some verbs it means "against" or "over". Learn the main meaning first and let reading teach you the rest.

## Practice

? Which case does a noun take after a preposition?
- u-case
- a-case
+ i-case
! Always. This is one of the most reliable rules in the language.

? What does فِي الْأَرْضِ mean?
+ in the earth
- from the earth
- to the earth
! فِي is "in", and الْأَرْضِ has the i-case ending that a preposition requires.

? Which is correct after مِنْ?
- مِنَ الْكِتَابُ
- مِنَ الْكِتَابَ
+ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ
! The noun must be in the i-case. The small a added to مِنْ is only there to make it easier to say before الـ.

? Why is بِـ written attached to the next word?
+ It is a single letter
- It only goes with names
- It is a verb
! One-letter words in Arabic are always joined to the word after them. The same goes for لِـ, كَـ and وَ.

- from Allah

- like Allah
! لِـ is "for" or "belonging to".

## Cards

Q: What case follows a preposition?
A: The i-case (مَجْرُورٌ), always.

Q: Which three prepositions are written joined to the next word?
A: بِـ (with, by), لِـ (for), كَـ (like).
