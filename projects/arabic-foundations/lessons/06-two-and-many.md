# Two, and many

English has singular and plural. Arabic has three numbers: one, exactly two, and three or more. This lesson covers the dual and the two regular plurals, all of which are made by adding an ending.

## The dual: exactly two

Add ـَانِ (-āni) to the singular. In the other two cases, which lesson 8 explains, it is ـَيْنِ (-ayni).

> رَجُلٌ    رَجُلَانِ
> a man, two men

> جَنَّةٌ    جَنَّتَانِ
> a garden, two gardens

Notice that ة opens up into an ordinary ت when an ending is added.

## The masculine plural

For groups of men, or mixed groups, add ـُونَ (-ūna). In the other two cases it is ـِينَ (-īna).

> مُؤْمِنٌ    مُؤْمِنُونَ    مُؤْمِنِينَ
> a believer, believers, believers

Both endings are everywhere in the Qur'an. الْعَالَمِينَ, "the worlds", has the -īna ending.

## The feminine plural

Drop the ة and add ـَاتٌ (-ātun).

> مُؤْمِنَةٌ    مُؤْمِنَاتٌ
> a believing woman, believing women

This ending is also used for many things that are not people: سَمَاوَاتٌ, "heavens", and كَلِمَاتٌ, "words".

These three are called **sound** endings because the singular stays whole inside them. Many nouns do not use them at all. Those are the subject of the next lesson.

## Cards

Q: What are the endings of the dual?
A: ـَانِ (-āni), and ـَيْنِ (-ayni) in the other two cases.

Q: What are the endings of the sound masculine plural?
A: ـُونَ (-ūna), and ـِينَ (-īna) in the other two cases.

Q: What is the ending of the sound feminine plural?
A: ـَاتٌ (-ātun), added after dropping the ة.
