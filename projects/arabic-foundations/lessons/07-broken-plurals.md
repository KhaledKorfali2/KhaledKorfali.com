# Broken plurals

Most Arabic nouns do not take a plural ending. Instead the word changes shape on the inside: the root letters stay, and the vowels around them are rearranged. These are called **broken plurals**, because the singular is broken apart to make them.

## What they look like

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|----------|--------|---------|
| كِتَابٌ | كُتُبٌ | book, books |
| رَسُولٌ | رُسُلٌ | messenger, messengers |
| قَلْبٌ | قُلُوبٌ | heart, hearts |
| يَوْمٌ | أَيَّامٌ | day, days |
| عَبْدٌ | عِبَادٌ | servant, servants |
| رَجُلٌ | رِجَالٌ | man, men |
| نَفْسٌ | أَنْفُسٌ | soul, souls |

English does the same thing in a handful of words: man and men, foot and feet. In Arabic it is the normal way.

## How to cope with them

There are a few dozen plural patterns, and you can't reliably predict which one a noun will use. Two things make this manageable:

- **Find the root.** كُتُبٌ still has ك ت ب in order, so you can recognise it as a form of كِتَابٌ even if you have never seen it before.
- **Learn each noun together with its plural**, the way you would learn "mouse, mice".

## One important rule

The plural of anything that is not a person is treated as **feminine singular**. An adjective or pronoun referring to "books" or "days" takes the feminine singular form. This explains a lot of feminine words in places where you might expect a plural.

## Practice

? What stays the same when a noun forms a broken plural?
+ Its root letters, in the same order
- Its vowels
- Its length
! The consonants of the root are kept. The vowels and sometimes extra letters change around them.

?sort Sound plural (an ending is added) or broken plural (the word changes shape)?
Sound: مُسْلِمُونَ, كَلِمَاتٌ, مُؤْمِنِينَ
Broken: كُتُبٌ, قُلُوبٌ, رِجَالٌ, أَيَّامٌ
! Sound plurals keep the singular whole and add -ūna, -īna or -ātun.

? You meet the unfamiliar plural عُلُومٌ. Which word is it most likely the plural of?
+ عِلْمٌ
- عَالَمٌ
- عَبْدٌ
! Look for the root: ع ل م, with the same pattern as قُلُوبٌ. It means "sciences, fields of knowledge".

## Cards

Q: What is a broken plural?
A: A plural made by changing the vowels inside the word while keeping the root letters, as in كِتَابٌ and كُتُبٌ.

Q: How does Arabic treat the plural of things that are not people?
A: As feminine singular.
