# Pronouns that attach

Far more often than they stand alone, Arabic pronouns are attached to the end of another word as a short ending. Where English uses three different words, "his", "him" and "it", Arabic uses one ending.

## The endings

| Ending | Say | Meaning |
|--------|-----|---------|
| ـهُ | -hu | his, him, it |
| ـهَا | -hā | her, it |
| ـهُمْ | -hum | their, them |
| ـكَ | -ka | your, you (one man) |
| ـكُمْ | -kum | your, you (a group) |
| ـِي | -ī | my |
| ـنَا | -nā | our, us |

## What they mean depends on what they join

**On a noun** they show the owner. The noun loses its tanwīn and can't take الـ, because belonging to someone already makes it definite.

> رَبٌّ    رَبُّهُمْ    رَبُّنَا
> a lord, their Lord, our Lord

**On a preposition** they are what the preposition points to.

> لَهُ    عَلَيْهِمْ    فِيهِ
> for him, upon them, in it

**On a verb** they are the object: the one the action is done to.

> خَلَقَكُمْ
> He created you.

## A change of sound

After an i sound or a ي, the endings ـهُ and ـهُمْ become ـهِ and ـهِمْ. This only makes them easier to say. The meaning is the same.

> بِهِ    فِيهِ    عَلَيْهِمْ
> with it, in it, upon them

## Practice

? What does رَبُّنَا mean?
- my Lord
+ our Lord
- their Lord
! ـنَا is "our" on a noun and "us" on a verb.

? What does the ending ـكُمْ mean when it is on a verb, as in خَلَقَكُمْ?
- your
+ you, as the object
- they
! On a verb an attached pronoun is the object: "He created you."

? Why is it فِيهِ and not فِيهُ?
+ ـهُ becomes ـهِ after an i sound or a ي
- Because it is feminine
- Because it is plural
! It is only a change of sound. The meaning is still "in it".

? كِتَابٌ means "a book". How do you say "his book"?
+ كِتَابُهُ
- الْكِتَابُهُ
- كِتَابٌهُ
! A noun with an attached pronoun takes neither الـ nor tanwīn.

? What does لَهُمْ mean?
+ for them
- for him
- from them
! لِـ is "for" and ـهُمْ is "them". Before a pronoun لِـ is pronounced la.

## Cards

Q: What does an attached pronoun mean on a noun, on a preposition and on a verb?
A: On a noun it is the owner (his book). On a preposition it is what the preposition points to (in it). On a verb it is the object (created you).

Q: When do ـهُ and ـهُمْ become ـهِ and ـهِمْ?
A: After an i sound or a ي, as in بِهِ, فِيهِ and عَلَيْهِمْ.
