# The past tense

The simplest form of an Arabic verb is "he did it" in the past. It is usually just the three root letters with short vowels: خَلَقَ, "he created". Dictionaries list verbs under this form, because nothing has been added to it.

## Who did it

The past tense shows who did the action with an **ending**. The start of the word doesn't change.

| Arabic | Say | Meaning |
|--------|-----|---------|
| خَلَقَ | khalaqa | he created |
| خَلَقَتْ | khalaqat | she created |
| خَلَقُوا | khalaqū | they created |
| خَلَقْتَ | khalaqta | you created (one man) |
| خَلَقْتُمْ | khalaqtum | you created (a group) |
| خَلَقْتُ | khalaqtu | I created |
| خَلَقْنَا | khalaqnā | we created |

Because the ending already says who did it, Arabic rarely needs a separate pronoun. خَلَقْنَا is a full sentence: "We created."

The alif written at the end of خَلَقُوا is silent. It is only a spelling convention for "they" verbs.

## In the Qur'an

> خَلَقَ الْإِنْسَانَ
> He created man. (55:3)

> الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ
> those who believed and did righteous deeds

> أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ
> You have bestowed favour upon them. (1:7)

Read the endings: ـُوا for "they" on آمَنُوا and عَمِلُوا, and ـْتَ for "you" on أَنْعَمْتَ.

- they
+ you

?sort Who did it?
He: خَلَقَ, قَالَ, كَفَرَ
They: خَلَقُوا, قَالُوا, كَفَرُوا
We: خَلَقْنَا, جَعَلْنَا

? Why is the form خَلَقَ used to list a verb in the dictionary?
+ It is the simplest form, with nothing added to the root
- It is the most polite form
- It is the present tense
! "He did it" in the past is the bare verb.

## Cards

Q: What are the past-tense endings for "they", "you (one man)", "I" and "we"?
A: ـُوا (they), ـْتَ (you), ـْتُ (I), ـْنَا (we).

Q: What is the simplest form of an Arabic verb?
A: "He did it" in the past, such as خَلَقَ. It is the form dictionaries use.
