# Reading al-Fātiḥah

You now have enough to read the opening chapter of the Qur'an and explain why each word has the shape it has. Go through it slowly. Every point here comes from an earlier lesson.

## Verse 1

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
> In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.

بِـ is a preposition, so اسْمِ is in the i-case. اسْمِ اللَّهِ is an "X of Y" phrase, so the name of Allah is in the i-case too. The two descriptions that follow match it: both are definite and both end in i.

## Verse 2

> الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
> All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds.

A nominal sentence. الْحَمْدُ is the subject, in the u-case. لِلَّهِ is the information. رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ is an "X of Y" phrase whose second word has the plural i-case ending ـِينَ.

## Verses 3 and 4

> الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
> مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ
> The Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgement.

More descriptions of Allah, all still in the i-case to match لِلَّهِ. مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ is a chain of three nouns, "X of Y of Z": the first two have no الـ and no tanwīn. مَالِك is on the doer pattern.

## Verse 5

> إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ
> You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.

Two present-tense verbs starting with نَـ, "we". إِيَّاكَ is the object, "You". An object normally follows its verb. Putting it first limits the action to it: You, and no one else.

## Verse 6

> اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ
> Guide us to the straight path.

A command with ـنَا, "us", attached. Then a noun and its adjective, matching in definiteness and both in the a-case.

## Verse 7

> صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ
> The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, not of those who have earned anger, nor of those who are astray.

صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ is "X of Y": the path of those who. أَنْعَمْتَ is past tense with the "you" ending. عَلَيْهِمْ is "upon them". الْمَغْضُوبِ is on the done-to pattern. الضَّالِّينَ is a doer-pattern word with the plural ending ـِينَ.

## Practice

?order Put the first verse in order.
بِسْمِ | اللَّهِ | الرَّحْمَٰنِ | الرَّحِيمِ
! A preposition, the noun it governs, the owner in an "X of Y" phrase, and two matching descriptions.

? Why is اللَّهِ in the i-case in بِسْمِ اللَّهِ?
+ It is the second noun of an "X of Y" phrase
- It follows a verb
- It is the subject
! "The name of Allah": the owner is always in the i-case.

?order Put the second verse in order.
الْحَمْدُ | لِلَّهِ | رَبِّ | الْعَالَمِينَ
! The subject, the information, then a description of Allah.

? What kind of sentence is الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ?
+ A nominal sentence, with no word for "is"
- A verbal sentence
- A command
! It starts with a noun and has no verb.

? In إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ, why does the object come before the verb?
+ To limit the worship to Him alone
- Because objects always come first
- Because نَعْبُدُ is a past-tense verb
! Moving the object to the front gives the meaning "You alone".

?order Put verse five in order.
إِيَّاكَ | نَعْبُدُ | وَإِيَّاكَ | نَسْتَعِينُ
! The object, a "we" verb, then the same again.

? What kind of word is اهْدِنَا?
- A past-tense verb with "we"
+ A command with "us" attached
- A noun with "our"
! اِهْدِ is the command "guide" and ـنَا is "us".

?order Put verse six in order.
اهْدِنَا | الصِّرَاطَ | الْمُسْتَقِيمَ
! The command, then the noun, then its adjective.

? In الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ, why do both words end in a?
+ The noun is an object, and the adjective matches it
- They follow a preposition
- They are both subjects
! An adjective copies the case of its noun.

?match Match each word of the last verse to what it is.
أَنْعَمْتَ = past tense, "you"
عَلَيْهِمْ = preposition with "them"
الْمَغْضُوبِ = done-to pattern
الَّذِينَ = "those who"

?sort Which lesson's rule explains each form?
Preposition, so i-case: بِسْمِ, لِلَّهِ
X of Y: رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ, يَوْمِ الدِّينِ
Verb starting with نَـ for "we": نَعْبُدُ, نَسْتَعِينُ

## Cards

Q: Why does إِيَّاكَ come before the verb in إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ?
A: Putting the object first limits the action to it: "You alone we worship."

Q: Why are الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ and مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ all in the i-case?
A: They describe Allah in لِلَّهِ, which is in the i-case after the preposition لِـ, and descriptions match the case of what they describe.
