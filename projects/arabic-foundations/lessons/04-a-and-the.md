# "A" and "the"

English puts "a" in front of a noun. Arabic puts a sound on the end instead. That sound is an "n", and it is written by doubling the final vowel mark. It is called **tanwīn**.

## Tanwīn

| Written | Sound | Example | Say |
|---------|-------|---------|-----|
| ـٌ | -un | كِتَابٌ | kitābun |
| ـً | -an | كِتَابًا | kitāban |
| ـٍ | -in | كِتَابٍ | kitābin |

All three mean "a book". Which of the three you get depends on the word's job in the sentence, which is the subject of lesson 8. For now, read any doubled mark as "a".

The -an form usually has an extra ا written after it, as in كِتَابًا. That alif is not pronounced as a long vowel.

## Definite or indefinite, never both

A noun takes either الـ or tanwīn. It never takes both.

> كِتَابٌ    الْكِتَابُ
> a book, the book

When الـ arrives, the doubled mark drops back to a single one: كِتَابٌ becomes الْكِتَابُ.

## Names

Names of people are definite by nature, yet many still carry tanwīn: مُحَمَّدٌ, نُوحٌ. That is simply how those names are pronounced. It does not make them mean "a Muhammad".

## Cards

Q: How does Arabic say "a", as in "a book"?
A: With tanwīn: an "n" sound on the end of the noun, written as a doubled vowel mark. كِتَابٌ is kitābun.

Q: Can a noun have both الـ and tanwīn?
A: No. It takes one or the other.
