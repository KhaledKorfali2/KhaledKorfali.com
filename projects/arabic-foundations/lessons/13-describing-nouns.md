# Describing a noun

In English the adjective comes first: "a straight path". In Arabic it comes **after** the noun, and it copies the noun in four ways.

## The four agreements

An adjective matches its noun in:

1. **Gender**: masculine or feminine
2. **Number**: singular, dual or plural
3. **Definiteness**: both have الـ, or neither does
4. **Case**: the same ending

> صِرَاطٌ مُسْتَقِيمٌ
> a straight path

> الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ
> the straight path (1:6)

In the second example both words have الـ and both end in a, because the phrase is the object of a verb.

## Why this is useful

Because the two words echo each other, an adjective phrase is easy to spot. When you see two words in a row with the same definiteness and the same ending, the second is very likely describing the first.

> عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ
> a tremendous punishment (2:7)

> أَجْرٌ كَرِيمٌ
> a generous reward

## Feminine nouns

The adjective takes ة to match a feminine noun: جَنَّةٌ كَبِيرَةٌ, "a great garden". And since plurals of things count as feminine singular, they take a feminine singular adjective too.
