# Masculine and feminine

Every Arabic noun is either masculine or feminine, including things that have no gender in real life. A book is masculine. A garden is feminine. The gender matters because adjectives, pronouns and verbs all have to match it.

## The feminine ending

Most feminine nouns end in ة. This letter is called **tāʾ marbūṭah**. It is pronounced "t" when you carry on to the next word and "h" when you stop on it.

> جَنَّةٌ    رَحْمَةٌ    آيَةٌ
> a garden, mercy, a sign

Many words for people come in pairs, and adding ة makes the feminine one:

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| مُؤْمِنٌ | مُؤْمِنَةٌ | a believer |
| كَافِرٌ | كَافِرَةٌ | a disbeliever |

## Feminine without the ending

A small group of common nouns are feminine with no ة. They have to be learned. Four that come up constantly:

> شَمْسٌ    أَرْضٌ    نَفْسٌ    نَارٌ
> sun, earth, soul, fire

A few others end in a long ā instead, such as الدُّنْيَا, "this world".

## If there is no sign, assume masculine

A noun with no feminine ending that isn't on the short list of exceptions is masculine: كِتَابٌ, قَلَمٌ, بَيْتٌ, يَوْمٌ.

## Practice

? What is the usual sign that a noun is feminine?
+ It ends in ة
- It begins with الـ
- It has tanwīn
! The tāʾ marbūṭah is the regular feminine ending.

?sort Masculine or feminine?
Masculine: كِتَابٌ, قَلَمٌ, بَيْتٌ, يَوْمٌ
Feminine: جَنَّةٌ, رَحْمَةٌ, صَلَاةٌ, آيَةٌ
! The four on the feminine side all end in ة.

? Which of these is feminine even though it has no ة?
- قَمَرٌ
+ شَمْسٌ
- نُورٌ
! شَمْسٌ is one of the common nouns that are feminine without the ending. The moon, قَمَرٌ, is masculine.

? What is the feminine of مُؤْمِنٌ?
+ مُؤْمِنَةٌ
- مُؤْمِنُونَ
- الْمُؤْمِنُ
! Add ة to make the feminine. The vowel before it becomes a.

? Which pair are both feminine?
+ نَارٌ and نَفْسٌ
- بَيْتٌ and نَارٌ
- قَلَمٌ and نَفْسٌ
! Fire and soul are both on the list of nouns that are feminine without ة.

? How is ة pronounced when you stop on the word?
- t
+ h
- it is silent
! جَنَّةٌ said on its own is "jannah". Carrying on, it is "jannatun".
