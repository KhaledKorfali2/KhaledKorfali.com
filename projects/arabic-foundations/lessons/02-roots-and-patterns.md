# Roots and patterns

This is the idea that makes Arabic vocabulary learnable. Most words are built from a **root** of three consonants, poured into a **pattern** of vowels and extra letters. The root carries the core meaning. The pattern says what kind of word it is.

## One root, many words

Take the root ك ت ب, which has to do with writing:

| Word | Say | Meaning |
|------|-----|---------|
| كَتَبَ | kataba | he wrote |
| كِتَابٌ | kitāb | a book |
| كَاتِبٌ | kātib | a writer |
| مَكْتُوبٌ | maktūb | written |

The three consonants k, t, b appear in the same order every time. Only the vowels and the added letters change.

## One pattern, many roots

Now keep the pattern and change the root. The pattern of كَاتِبٌ has a long ā after the first root letter and an i after the second. It means "the one who does it":

- ك ت ب gives كَاتِبٌ, a writer.
- ع ل م (knowing) gives عَالِمٌ, one who knows, a scholar.

So when you meet a new word, look for its three root letters. If you know the root and recognise the pattern, you can often work out the meaning before you look it up.

> الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
> Both words come from the root ر ح م, which is about mercy. They are two different patterns on one root.

## Practice

? What does the root of a word carry?
+ Its core meaning
- Its tense
- Whether it is definite
! The root gives the basic idea. The pattern around it decides the kind of word.

? What is the root of مَكْتُوبٌ?
+ ك ت ب
- م ك ت
- ت و ب
! The م at the start and the و in the middle belong to the pattern. The root letters are ك ت ب.

?sort Sort the words by their root.
ك ت ب: كِتَابٌ, كَاتِبٌ, مَكْتُوبٌ
ع ل م: عِلْمٌ, عَالِمٌ, عَلِيمٌ
ر ح م: رَحْمَةٌ, رَحِيمٌ

? كَاتِبٌ means "a writer" and عَالِمٌ means "one who knows". The root س ج د is about prostrating. What would سَاجِدٌ mean?
- a place of prostration
+ one who prostrates
- prostrated upon
! Same pattern, same idea: the one who does the action of the root.

? رَحِيمٌ means "merciful". Which word shares its pattern?
+ عَلِيمٌ
- عَالِمٌ
- عِلْمٌ
! Both have an a after the first root letter and a long ī after the second.

## Cards

Q: What is a root in Arabic?
A: Usually three consonants, always in the same order, that carry a word's core meaning. Example: ك ت ب for writing.

Q: What does a pattern add to a root?
A: The vowels and extra letters that decide what kind of word it is, such as "the one who does it" in كَاتِبٌ and عَالِمٌ.
