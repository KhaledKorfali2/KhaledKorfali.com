# Sentences without "is"

Arabic has no word for "is" or "are" in the present. To say that something is something, you put the two side by side. This is called a **nominal sentence**, because it starts with a noun.

## Two parts

- The **subject**: what you are talking about. It is definite and in the u-case.
- The **information**: what you say about it. It is usually indefinite, also in the u-case.

> اللَّهُ غَفُورٌ
> Allah is forgiving.

> وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ
> And Allah is all-knowing, wise.

Nothing is missing from these. The gap between a definite word and an indefinite one is where English says "is".

## Phrase or sentence?

Compare these two.

| Arabic | Meaning | Why |
|--------|---------|-----|
| الرَّبُّ الْغَفُورُ | the forgiving Lord | Both definite: an adjective phrase |
| الرَّبُّ غَفُورٌ | The Lord is forgiving. | Definite then indefinite: a sentence |

One الـ changes a phrase into a complete statement. This is the single most useful thing to watch for when you read.

## The information can be a phrase

The second part doesn't have to be one word. Very often it is a preposition with its noun.

> الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ
> All praise is for Allah. (1:2)

Here الْحَمْدُ is the subject and لِلَّهِ is the information.

## Practice

? How does Arabic say "is" in a sentence like "Allah is forgiving"?
- With the word هُوَ
+ It doesn't. The two parts are placed side by side
- With a special ending on the first word
! A nominal sentence has no verb.

? What does الرَّبُّ غَفُورٌ mean?
- the forgiving Lord
+ The Lord is forgiving.
- a forgiving lord
! Definite followed by indefinite makes a sentence.

? What does الرَّبُّ الْغَفُورُ mean?
+ the forgiving Lord
- The Lord is forgiving.
- The Lord forgave.
! Both words are definite, so the second describes the first. It is a phrase, not a sentence.

?sort Phrase or complete sentence?
Phrase: الْكِتَابُ الْكَرِيمُ, الرَّبُّ الْغَفُورُ, صِرَاطٌ مُسْتَقِيمٌ
Sentence: اللَّهُ غَفُورٌ, الْكِتَابُ كَرِيمٌ, الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ
! When the two words match in definiteness it is a phrase. Definite then indefinite, or definite then a preposition phrase, is a sentence.

? Which case are both parts of a nominal sentence normally in?
+ u-case
- a-case
- i-case
! Subject and information are both in the u-case, unless a word in front changes that. The next lesson covers those words.

?order Put the words in order: "And Allah is all-knowing, wise."
وَاللَّهُ | عَلِيمٌ | حَكِيمٌ
! The subject first, then two pieces of information about it.

? In الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, what is the information?
- الْحَمْدُ
+ لِلَّهِ
! الْحَمْدُ is what the sentence is about. لِلَّهِ, "for Allah", is what is said about it.

## Cards

Q: How do you tell an adjective phrase from a nominal sentence?
A: If both words match in definiteness it is a phrase (الرَّبُّ الْغَفُورُ, the forgiving Lord). Definite then indefinite is a sentence (الرَّبُّ غَفُورٌ, The Lord is forgiving).
