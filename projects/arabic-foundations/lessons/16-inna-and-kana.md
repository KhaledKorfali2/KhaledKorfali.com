# إِنَّ and كَانَ

Two small words are placed in front of nominal sentences all the time. Each one adds a shade of meaning, and each one changes a case ending. Once you expect the change, it stops looking like a mistake.

## إِنَّ: "indeed"

إِنَّ adds emphasis. It puts the **subject** into the a-case. The information stays in the u-case.

> اللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ
> Allah is forgiving, merciful.

> إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ
> Indeed, Allah is forgiving, merciful.

Look at the name of Allah: it ends in u in the first sentence and in a in the second.

إِنَّ has several sisters that do the same thing to the subject, including أَنَّ, "that", لَٰكِنَّ, "but", and لَعَلَّ, "perhaps, so that".

## كَانَ: "was"

كَانَ is a verb meaning "was". It does the opposite: the subject stays in the u-case and the **information** goes into the a-case.

> وَكَانَ اللَّهُ غَفُورًا رَحِيمًا
> And Allah is ever forgiving, merciful. (4:96)

When كَانَ is used about Allah, it does not mean something that was true and has stopped. It describes what has always been so, which is why translations often say "is ever".

Its sister لَيْسَ, "is not", works the same way.

## Side by side

| In front | Subject | Information |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| nothing | u-case | u-case |
| إِنَّ | **a-case** | u-case |
| كَانَ | u-case | **a-case** |

## Words

| Word | Say | Meaning | Note |
|------|-----|---------|------|
| إِنَّ | inna | indeed | Puts the subject in the a-case |
| أَنَّ | anna | that | A sister of إِنَّ |
| لَٰكِنَّ | lākinna | but | A sister of إِنَّ |
| لَعَلَّ | laʿalla | perhaps, so that | A sister of إِنَّ |
| كَانَ | kāna | he was | Puts the information in the a-case |
| لَيْسَ | laysa | is not | A sister of كَانَ |
| قَدْ | qad | already, certainly | Used before a verb |
| بَلْ | bal | rather | Corrects what came before |

## Practice

? What does إِنَّ do to the subject of a nominal sentence?
+ Puts it in the a-case
- Puts it in the i-case
- Makes it indefinite
! The subject takes an a ending. The information is unchanged.

? Which is correct?
- إِنَّ اللَّهُ غَفُورٌ
+ إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ
- إِنَّ اللَّهِ غَفُورٌ
! After إِنَّ the subject is in the a-case.

? What does كَانَ do?
- Puts the subject in the a-case
+ Puts the information in the a-case
- Puts both in the i-case
! كَانَ leaves the subject alone and changes the information.

? Which is correct after كَانَ?
+ كَانَ اللَّهُ غَفُورًا
- كَانَ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ
- كَانَ اللَّهُ غَفُورٌ
! The subject keeps its u ending and the information takes -an.

?sort Which word caused the ending? Look at which part is in the a-case.
إِنَّ: إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ, إِنَّ الْكِتَابَ كَرِيمٌ
كَانَ: كَانَ اللَّهُ عَلِيمًا, كَانَ الْكِتَابُ كَرِيمًا
! With إِنَّ the first noun ends in a. With كَانَ the last word does.

## Cards

Q: What does إِنَّ do to a nominal sentence?
A: It adds emphasis ("indeed") and puts the subject in the a-case: إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ.

Q: What does كَانَ do to a nominal sentence?
A: It means "was" and puts the information in the a-case: كَانَ اللَّهُ غَفُورًا.
