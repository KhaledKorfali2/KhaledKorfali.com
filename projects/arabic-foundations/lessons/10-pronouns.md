# Pronouns that stand alone

These are the words for "I", "you", "he" and so on when they stand as separate words. Arabic has more of them than English, because it separates masculine from feminine and two from many.

## The ones to know first

| Arabic | Say | Meaning |
|--------|-----|---------|
| هُوَ | huwa | he, it |
| هِيَ | hiya | she, it |
| هُمْ | hum | they (men, or a mixed group) |
| هُنَّ | hunna | they (women) |
| أَنْتَ | anta | you (one man) |
| أَنْتُمْ | antum | you (a group) |
| أَنَا | anā | I |
| نَحْنُ | naḥnu | we |

There are also forms for "you" said to one woman (أَنْتِ), to two people (أَنْتُمَا), to a group of women (أَنْتُنَّ) and for "the two of them" (هُمَا). They follow the same shapes and you will recognise them when they appear.

## In use

> قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
> Say: He is Allah, One. (112:1)

> وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ
> And those are the successful. (2:5)

A standalone pronoun is most often the subject of a sentence. Notice that there is no word for "is" in either example. Arabic sentences of this kind don't need one, as lesson 15 explains.

## "It"

Arabic has no separate word for "it". A masculine thing is هُوَ and a feminine thing is هِيَ. Plurals of things that aren't people are feminine singular, so they are هِيَ too.

## Practice

? Which pronoun would you use for a group of men, or a mixed group?
- هُوَ
- نَحْنُ
+ هُمْ
! هُمْ is "they". A group made up only of women is هُنَّ.

? Which pronoun would stand for جَنَّةٌ, "a garden"?
- هُوَ
+ هِيَ
- هُمْ
! جَنَّةٌ is feminine, so "it" is هِيَ.

? Which pronoun would stand for كُتُبٌ, "books"?
- هُمْ
- هُوَ
+ هِيَ
! Plurals of things that are not people are treated as feminine singular.
