# The three cases

In English, word order tells you who did what: "the man saw the king" and "the king saw the man" mean different things. Arabic marks each noun's job with the **vowel on its last letter** instead. That ending is the noun's case.

There are three cases.

| Case | Arabic name | Ending | Used for |
|------|-------------|--------|----------|
| The u-case | مَرْفُوعٌ | ـُ or ـٌ | The subject of a sentence |
| The a-case | مَنْصُوبٌ | ـَ or ـً | The object of a verb |
| The i-case | مَجْرُورٌ | ـِ or ـٍ | After a preposition, and for the owner in "X of Y" |

So "the book" can appear as الْكِتَابُ, الْكِتَابَ or الْكِتَابِ. It is the same word doing three different jobs.

## Reading the cases in a verse

> الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
> All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds. (1:2)

- الْحَمْدُ ends in u. It is the subject: praise is what the sentence is about.
- لِلَّهِ ends in i, because it follows the preposition لِـ, "for".
- رَبِّ ends in i, because it describes the word before it and so takes the same case.
- الْعَالَمِينَ has the -īna ending, which is how a masculine plural shows the i-case. It is the owner in "Lord of the worlds".

## Endings that are not a single vowel

You have already met these. Now you know what the two forms were for.

| | u-case | a-case and i-case |
|---|--------|-------------------|
| Dual | ـَانِ | ـَيْنِ |
| Masculine plural | ـُونَ | ـِينَ |

The case endings are what the rest of this course builds on. Every later lesson says which case a word takes and why.

## Practice

? What shows a noun's job in an Arabic sentence?
- Its position in the sentence
+ The vowel on its last letter
- Whether it has الـ
! The case ending marks the role, which is why Arabic word order can be flexible.

? Which case does the subject of a sentence take?
+ مَرْفُوعٌ
- مَنْصُوبٌ
- مَجْرُورٌ
! The subject is in the u-case.

?sort Sort each word by its case ending.
u-case: الْكِتَابُ, رَسُولٌ, الْحَمْدُ
a-case: الْكِتَابَ, رَسُولًا, الصِّرَاطَ
i-case: الْكِتَابِ, رَسُولٍ, الدِّينِ

? In لِلَّهِ, why does the word end in i?
+ It follows a preposition
- It is the subject
- It is feminine
! لِـ means "for". A noun after a preposition is always in the i-case.

? Which form of "the believers" is in the u-case?
+ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ
- الْمُؤْمِنِينَ
! -ūna is the u-case. -īna covers both the a-case and the i-case.

?match Match each case to its job.
مَرْفُوعٌ = the subject
مَنْصُوبٌ = the object of a verb
مَجْرُورٌ = after a preposition

? In الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ, which word is the subject?
> الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
+ الْحَمْدُ
- رَبِّ
- الْعَالَمِينَ
! It is the only word in the u-case.

## Cards

Q: What are the three cases, and their endings?
A: مَرْفُوعٌ (u), مَنْصُوبٌ (a) and مَجْرُورٌ (i).

Q: When is a noun in the i-case (مَجْرُورٌ)?
A: After a preposition, and when it is the owner in an "X of Y" phrase.
