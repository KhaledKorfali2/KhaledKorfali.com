# This, that, and who

Two small families of words tie Arabic sentences together: words that point, and words that link a description onto a noun. Both change with gender and number.

## Words that point

| Arabic | Say | Meaning |
|--------|-----|---------|
| هَٰذَا | hādhā | this (masculine) |
| هَٰذِهِ | hādhihi | this (feminine) |
| ذَٰلِكَ | dhālika | that (masculine) |
| تِلْكَ | tilka | that (feminine) |
| أُولَٰئِكَ | ulāʾika | those (people) |

The small upright stroke in هَٰذَا and ذَٰلِكَ is a long ā that is pronounced but not written as a full alif.

> ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ
> That is the Book in which there is no doubt. (2:2)

Remember that plurals of things are feminine singular, so "these verses" uses a feminine singular pointing word: تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ, "Those are the verses of the Book".

## Words that link

These mean "who", "which" or "that", and attach a description to a definite noun.

| Arabic | Say | Meaning |
|--------|-----|---------|
| الَّذِي | alladhī | who, which (masculine singular) |
| الَّتِي | allatī | who, which (feminine singular) |
| الَّذِينَ | alladhīna | those who (plural) |

> الَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِالْغَيْبِ
> those who believe in the unseen (2:3)

الَّذِينَ is one of the most frequent words in the Qur'an. Whenever you see it, expect a description of a group of people to follow.

Two more linking words work for any gender or number: مَنْ for people, "whoever", and مَا for things, "whatever".

## Cards

Q: Why does تِلْكَ, a feminine singular word, appear before plural nouns such as "verses"?
A: Because plurals of things that aren't people are treated as feminine singular.

Q: What does الَّذِينَ mean?
A: "Those who": it introduces a description of a group of people.
