# "X of Y"

To say that one thing belongs to another, Arabic puts the two nouns side by side: the thing owned first, the owner second. English "the Messenger of Allah" and "Allah's Messenger" are both expressed this way. The construction is called **iḍāfah**.

## The two rules

> رَسُولُ اللَّهِ
> the Messenger of Allah

1. **The first noun has no الـ and no tanwīn.** It is "light", stripped of both.
2. **The second noun is always in the i-case.** It is the owner.

The first noun takes whatever case its job in the sentence calls for. The second is in the i-case regardless.

## Examples

> رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
> Lord of the worlds (1:2)

> يَوْمِ الدِّينِ
> the Day of Judgement (1:4)

> لَيْلَةِ الْقَدْرِ
> the Night of Decree (97:1)

In each of these the first word has neither الـ nor tanwīn, and the second word has an i-case ending.

## Telling it apart from an adjective phrase

Both are two nouns in a row, so look at the first word and at the endings.

| | Example | First word | Second word |
|---|---------|------------|-------------|
| Adjective phrase | الْكِتَابُ الْكَرِيمُ | matches the second | same case as the first |
| X of Y | كِتَابُ اللَّهِ | no الـ, no tanwīn | always i-case |

The first means "the noble book". The second means "the Book of Allah".

## It is definite if the owner is

كِتَابُ اللَّهِ means "the Book of Allah", not "a book of Allah", even though كِتَابُ has no الـ. The first noun takes its definiteness from the second.

## Cards

Q: What are the two rules of an "X of Y" phrase (iḍāfah)?
A: The first noun has no الـ and no tanwīn. The second noun is always in the i-case.

Q: Is كِتَابُ اللَّهِ definite or indefinite?
A: Definite: "the Book of Allah". The first noun takes its definiteness from the second.
